    Odometry

    3.0

    Worn treads, carpet seams and wheel scrub make each wheel's distance a little wrong. Those errors add up the farther you drive. Pushing into the hub, a trench wall or the guardrail makes it worse, because the wheels keep turning while the robot doesn't move.

    15

    On a bump, the wheels roll along a 16° slope, so they count about 4% more distance than the robot covers across the floor. They also skid as the robot tips over the peak.

    Cameras

    ×1.0

    How far each reading lands from the truth. It grows with tag distance, with how sharply the camera sees the tag, and with motion blur from driving or spinning during the camera's exposure.

    Alliance
    Tags the Limelights use

    The 2026 robot's TagFilterMode options. The Limelights ignore every tag outside the list, and those tags are grayed out on the field.

    Three Limelight 4s sit about 8" off the floor, facing left, right and rear, pitched up 23°. Each sees 82° side to side and 56° up and down. Because they tilt up, a tag that is too close or too low drops out of view. The hub and trench walls block the view of anything behind them.

    Kalman filter

    Each accepted reading pulls the estimate a fraction q ÷ (q + σ) of the way toward it. That's the WPILib pose estimator's gain. A close multi-tag reading (σ 0.15 m) pulls 40% of the way. A far single-tag reading (σ 5 m) pulls 2%.

    CameraTagsDistAreaResult

    Tag area is the share of the image a tag covers. A tag 3 m away covers about 0.16%.

    Why each reading is off

    Expected error (1σ) = distance error × viewing-angle factor + motion blur + bump tilt. The robot never sees this number. It only knows tag area, which is why the 2026 tables can't tell a sharp or blurry reading from a clean one.
